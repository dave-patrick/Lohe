Getting started

Find a show, shape your listening queue, and make Lohe fit the way you listen.

Install the current preview

  1. Open the Lohe 0.10.1 GitHub release and download app-release.apk.
  2. Open the APK on your Android device. If Android asks, allow your browser or file manager to install apps.
  3. Launch Lohe and add a podcast from Discover, or import an OPML subscription file from your podcast library.

This is a signed Android release distributed through GitHub. It is not listed on Google Play.

Find and play episodes

  • My Podcasts contains subscribed shows and their episodes.
  • Discover searches the podcast catalog so you can find and subscribe to shows.
  • Tap an episode to play it, or add it to Up Next to build a listening queue.
  • Drag an episode by its grip in Up Next to change the order. A show's settings can add new episodes at the beginning or end of the queue.
  • Playback continues in the background. The notification and Android media controls provide basic transport actions.

When an episode finishes, Lohe marks it played and removes it from explicit playlists and Up Next. It remains in the Played smart playlist. Its downloaded audio follows the archive delay in Settings → Archiving (24 hours by default).

Tune playback

Open Settings → Playback to set skip intervals, default playback speed, volume boost, voice enhancement, silence handling, and other playback defaults. The player also has episode-level controls. Set a sleep timer from the player when you want playback to stop after a chosen time.

Settings → Floating player enables optional playback controls over other apps. Android requires the separate Display over other apps permission; the floating player starts off.

Organize your listening

Create playlists in Playlists, add episodes, and arrange them in the order you want. Grid and list views are available. Smart playlists collect played, in-progress, unplayed, downloaded, and recently released episodes automatically.

Android Auto

Lohe exposes its podcast library and playback through Android's media browser, which Android Auto uses to browse and play audio. Android Automotive OS is a separate system; its in-car screen integration has not yet been verified on a head unit.
